Who can use CRA My Account?
    
Anyone with a social insurance number (SIN) and a filed and assessed income tax and benefit return from the current or previous tax year. That is the test on the CRA's About My Account page. If you have no SIN, the registration page also accepts a temporary tax number or an individual tax number.
    
The practical effect is timing. If you filed your first return this spring, the account was out of reach until the notice of assessment came back. If you skipped filing for the last two years, you will have to file first.
    
My Account is one door into a CRA account that also holds My Business Account, Represent a Client and other accounts. You register once and use the same sign-in for all of them, according to the CRA's registration page.

    
How do you register for a CRA account?
    
In five steps, and the first one is the one people skip: gather what you need before you start, because the session expires after 18 minutes of inactivity and nothing is saved. The CRA's registration page lists the steps.
    

        
1Gather your details. Your date of birth and SIN, plus amounts you reported on your most recent assessed return from the last 2 years. Use the amounts you first reported, even if a reassessment later changed them.
        
2Choose a registration option. CRA user ID and password, a Sign-In Partner, or a provincial partner. The next section compares them.
        
3Enroll in multi-factor authentication. A one-time passcode is required at every sign-in. First-time registrants are prompted to enrol in 2 options.
        
4Verify your identity. With the document verification service on a mobile device, or with a security code mailed to you.
        
5Add other accounts. Business, representative and other accounts can be added after you sign in.
    
    
For immediate access you also need a mobile device with a camera and an accepted piece of ID. Without one, plan around the mail.

    
Which sign-in option should you choose?
    
The one you will actually remember, because the CRA says the option you register with is the one you use every time you sign in. There are three, and GC Key is not one of them: the CRA states you cannot use GC Key to sign in to your CRA account.
    

        

            The three CRA account sign-in options (CRA, read November 26, 2026; the last column is our own view)
            
Option | How it works | Best for | 
            
                
CRA user ID and password | You create a username and password just for the CRA | People who want the CRA login separate from their bank, and who use a password manager | 
                
Sign-In Partner | You use your online banking sign-in at a participating bank or credit union | People who already sign in to a participating institution every week | 
                
Provincial partner | You use a BC Services Card or an Alberta.ca Account | British Columbia and Alberta residents who already have one | 
            
        
    
    
The CRA's sign-in page lists the current Sign-In Partners, and the list includes RBC Royal Bank, TD Bank Group, Scotiabank, BMO Financial Group, CIBC, National Bank of Canada, Desjardins, Tangerine, Simplii Financial and Wealthsimple, among others. Check that your institution is on it before choosing this route.

A warning about banking credentials. The CRA tells you not to use autofill on a shared device. If you register with someone else's banking credentials by mistake and link your SIN to them, the other person gets access to your tax information.

    
How do you verify your identity?
    
Choose between two methods. The document verification service gives full, immediate access. The CRA security code is mailed to the address the CRA has on file within 10 business days, and it expires, so use it as soon as it arrives. The CRA's verify your identity page sets out both.
    

        
Document verification service: you must be at least 16 years old, have an accepted government-issued photo ID and a mobile device with a working camera. Accepted documents include a Canadian driver's licence, a Canadian passport, a permanent resident card and a provincial or territorial photo ID card. Provincial health cards are not accepted.
        
CRA security code: you enter your postal code, and if it does not match the CRA's record you have to update your address first. If the code has not arrived after 10 business days, use the document verification service or contact the CRA for a new one.

What is multi-factor authentication and which method should you pick?
    
It is a one-time passcode you enter in addition to your password at every sign-in, and it is mandatory for all CRA account users. The CRA offers three methods, and you should enrol in at least two, even though only one is required.
    

        
Third-party authenticator app: a free app that makes a 6-digit code, renewed every 30 seconds. You do not need to pay for one.
        
Passcode grid: a grid the CRA generates for you to save or print.
        
Phone: a code sent by text or phone call. If you use a VoIP service, choose the Call me option instead of Text me.
    
    
The CRA says a backup protects you if you change your phone number, cannot reach your text messages, misplace the grid or delete the app. After you sign in, the Welcome page lets you add or change a method. Never read a passcode to anyone: the CRA says it will never call and ask for your one-time passcode.

    
What if you are locked out or forgot your sign-in?
    
You may be able to regain access without calling. The CRA's sign-in page says users locked out because they forgot their credentials can register again for a new credential. If you forgot your user ID, the recovery steps need your SIN, your date of birth, the amount you entered on line 15000 of your most recent return from one of the previous 2 tax years, and the answers to your security questions.
    

        
Forgot your password: choose CRA user ID and password, select Forgot your password, enter your user ID and answer your security questions.
        
No passcode arrived: ask for it again, send it to another number you registered, or fall back to your grid or app.
        
Account locked: it locks after too many wrong entries, and the CRA's help page lists the steps.
    
    
Be sceptical of any message that says your account is suspended. The CRA states it will not send you an email with a link asking you to sign in, and it keeps a scam alerts page for fake websites and messages. Type the address yourself or use the Canada.ca link instead.

    
What should you check in your CRA account each year?
    
Six things, in this order. The CRA's October 29, 2026 tax tip tells taxpayers to register or confirm they can sign in before tax season, and says a working account helps you use pre-filled returns, Auto-fill My Return in tax software and secure NETFILE or EFILE authentication when you file a 2026 return.

Can you use My Account for someone else?
    
Not with your own sign-in. To see a friend's or family member's information you must be their authorized representative and use Represent a Client in your own CRA account. The CRA also says My Account cannot be used to access a deceased person's information: an executor uses Represent a Client for that. Our guide to the final return for a deceased person covers what the executor files.

Frequently asked questions
    
Is CRA My Account the same as a CRA account?
    
No. Your CRA account is the sign-in that opens several services. My Account is the personal one, for your own income tax and benefits.
    
How long does it take to get access to My Account?
    
Immediately with the document verification service. By mail, the CRA security code arrives within 10 business days, and you get limited access to My Account until you enter it.
    
Can I register if I have never filed a tax return?
    
Not for My Account. You need an assessed return from the current or previous tax year, so file first and register after the notice of assessment arrives.
    
Can I use GC Key to sign in to My Account?
    
No. The CRA says GC Key can be used with other federal departments but not to sign in to your CRA account.
    
What if my security code expired?
    
Use the document verification service instead, or sign in to request a new code by mail.
    
Can I see my RRSP limit before my identity is verified?
    
Yes. The CRA lists your RRSP deduction limit and TFSA contribution room among the limited-access services.
